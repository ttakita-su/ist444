Where to Start With South Park: The Mogging Episode and Eight Classics

Twenty-nine seasons is a lot of homework. Start with the newest episode, then work back through the classics, from Casa Bonita to Imaginationland.

By Twisted Takes · October 20, 2026

Sample article. This is starter text written to launch Twisted Takes. The team has not yet reviewed it or approved it as its own opinion.

South Park has been on the air since 1997, which makes “just start at the beginning” terrible advice. Most episodes stand on their own, so you can dip in anywhere. Here’s the newest one worth your time, then eight classics that explain why the show has lasted. Every season streams on Paramount+ in the US, and the series is rated TV-MA, so maybe not with the kids in the room.

The newest: “You Like That On Your Face” (season 29, episode 3, 2026)

Aura battles and face-rating apps take over the school, Ike gets drawn into a looksmaxxing rivalry, and a trip to Sephora goes very, very wrong. It’s the best episode of the new season so far and a perfect example of the show catching an internet trend while it’s still happening. Our season 29 review has the full take.

“Scott Tenorman Must Die” (season 5, episode 4, 2001)

A ninth grader cons Cartman out of $16.12, and Cartman sets out to get even. That’s all you should know going in. It routinely tops best-episode lists because it’s the moment the show revealed just how far Cartman will go.

“The Return of the Fellowship of the Ring to the Two Towers” (season 6, episode 13, 2002)

The boys turn a simple errand, returning a rented tape, into a full Middle-earth quest, complete with a very much not-for-kids mix-up. It’s a loving parody that works best if you’ve seen the films; our Fellowship of the Ring review is a good warm-up. Parker and Stone have called it one of their favorites.

“Casa Bonita” (season 7, episode 11, 2003)

Kyle’s birthday trip to Casa Bonita, a real Mexican restaurant in Lakewood, Colorado, famous for its cliff divers and indoor waterfall, doesn’t include Cartman, so Cartman schemes his way back onto the guest list. The episode’s love for the place turned out to be sincere: when the restaurant went through bankruptcy in 2021, Trey Parker and Matt Stone bought it, restored it and reopened it in 2023. The documentary ¡Casa Bonita Mi Amor! (2024), also on Paramount+, tells that story.

“Good Times with Weapons” (season 8, episode 1, 2004)

The boys buy martial-arts weapons at a fair and play ninjas, and the show switches to anime style every time they’re in their own heads. It’s the series at its most visually playful, and a reminder that the cutout look can stretch further than you’d think.

“Marjorine” (season 9, episode 9, 2005)

The boys fake Butters’ death so he can go undercover, dressed as a new girl named Marjorine, at a girls’ slumber party. Butters is the show’s secret weapon, and this is his showcase. Watch for the old farmer and his Pet Sematary-style warnings, because he turns up again in the new season.

“Make Love, Not Warcraft” (season 10, episode 8, 2006)

A griefer keeps killing the boys’ characters in World of Warcraft, and they commit to stopping him at any cost. The game scenes were made with Blizzard’s help, using the game itself, and the episode won the 2007 Emmy for Outstanding Animated Program (Less Than One Hour). Gamers will feel very seen.

“Imaginationland” (season 11, episodes 10–12, 2007)

A bet about a leprechaun sends the boys into a world where every imaginary character lives, from heroes to nightmares. It’s a three-part story best watched in one sitting, almost a movie, and it won the 2008 Emmy for Outstanding Animated Program (For Programming One Hour or More).

“Sermon on the ‘Mount” (season 27, episode 1, 2025)

When the town faces possible ruin, Jesus returns with an important message. This premiere launched 2025’s all-out Trump satire, drew the show’s biggest season-premiere audience share since 1999, and won the 2026 Emmy for Outstanding Animated Program. If you want to know why South Park was suddenly in the news again, start here.

How to watch

If you’re brand new, try “Make Love, Not Warcraft” or “Imaginationland” first: they’re the easiest to enjoy without knowing the characters. If you already know Cartman, go straight to “Scott Tenorman Must Die.” And if you just want to know what everyone’s talking about this month, it’s the mogging episode.
