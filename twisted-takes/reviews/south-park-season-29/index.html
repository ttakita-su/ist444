South Park's 29th Season Still Speaks Fluent Internet, Mogging and All

South Park (2026), season 29

By Twisted Takes · October 20, 2026 · Spoiler-free

Content advisory: Crude sexual humor, nudity, gory cartoon violence, strong language and deliberately offensive jokes about real people and groups. Not for kids, whatever the animation suggests.

Reviewed through episode 3, “You Like That On Your Face,” which aired October 14, 2026. New episodes air every other Wednesday through November 25.

Premise

Twenty-nine seasons in, Stan, Kyle, Cartman and Kenny are still fourth graders in a small Colorado mountain town, which has now officially renamed itself South America. In the newest episode, aura battles and face-rating apps sweep the school, and Kyle tries to stop his little brother Ike from being, in the episode’s word, cringe.

The Take

After two 2025 seasons built around Trump satire, season 29 spreads its aim wider: e-bike gangs in the premiere, billionaires and their data centers invading Butters’ backyard in episode 2, and then the mogging episode, the best of the three. South Park has always been fastest when it treats a trend like the apocalypse, and because each episode is still made in about a week, it can catch an internet obsession while it’s still happening. “You Like That On Your Face” turns mogging, mewing and looksmaxxing into a full-blown end-times panic, and the result is the show at its most precise.

What Works

The episode keeps three plots spinning: Ike’s aura battle, Stan and his dad Randy running their faces through a “Chadmaxx” app that conveniently sells the fix, and a trip to Sephora that turns into a parody of the Backrooms, the internet’s endless-empty-hallway horror meme. That Backrooms stretch is the funniest thing the season has done, and the return of the creepy old farmer from 2005’s “Marjorine,” now warning Stan away from the makeup aisle, is a gift for longtime fans. Cartman, cheerfully stepping in as Ike’s supportive big brother while Kyle fumbles the job, is never more menacing than when he’s being nice. Under the gross-out jokes is a real point about kids, and parents, grading their own faces.

What May Divide Viewers

It’s still South Park: crude, gory in a cartoon way and happy to punch in every direction, sometimes at people who don’t deserve it. Episode 2 leans hard on full-frontal nudity. The rapid-fire slang (mogging, mewing, rizz, aura) will be a punchline for some viewers and a foreign language for others. And anyone who loved last year’s head-on political combat may find this season’s targets smaller.

Who Should Watch

Adults (the TV-MA rating is earned) who are online enough to know what mogging means, or who have a teenager who is and want to understand it. Lapsed fans can jump straight in: episode 3 works on its own. If you’ve never watched, start with our guide to iconic South Park episodes. Skip it if crude humor and no-sacred-cows satire aren’t for you.
