How to Lose a Guy in 10 Days Plays Romance Like a Rigged Card Game

How to Lose a Guy in 10 Days (2003)

Content advisory: Plenty of sexual innuendo, a few suggestive scenes and occasional swearing.

Premise

Magazine columnist Andie wants to write serious stories, but her next article requires dating a man and driving him off within ten days. Ad executive Ben bets he can make a woman fall for him in that same window. They end up dating each other, both hiding an agenda.

The Take

The double con is a clever engine. Because you know both secrets, every date plays like a duel: each clingy stunt from Andie is an attack, and each time Ben swallows it, he’s protecting his wager.

What Works

Kate Hudson commits to being exhausting, down to a “love fern” she insists they share, and never winks to apologize. Matthew McConaughey plays the straight man, his grin cracking a little more with each test. The best stretch is a weekend with Ben’s family on Staten Island, where a rowdy card game loosens everyone up.

What May Divide Viewers

Andie’s sabotage playbook leans on tired early-2000s clichés about “crazy girlfriends.” At nearly two hours, it also runs long for such a tidy premise.

Who Should Watch

Good for a low-key night with friends who want something breezy to talk over, especially if you like two stubborn people refusing to blink first.

Your take

Since you know both secrets from the start, do Andie and Ben's lies make the romance more fun or harder to root for?
