The Tinder Swindler Shows How a Jet-Set Fantasy Gets Sold One Text at a Time

The Tinder Swindler (2022)

Content advisory: Romance fraud as the women describe it: emotional manipulation, threatening messages, deep distress and strong language.

Premise

Several women describe meeting a charming, seemingly ultra-rich man on Tinder. The film traces how those relationships moved from private jets to urgent pleas for cash, using their saved messages, voice notes and photos.

The Take

Starting out as a dating-app fairy tale is the film’s smartest move. You feel the lifestyle’s pull before any red flags appear, so the smug “how could anyone fall for this” reaction is hard to keep. It is persuasion, shown from the inside.

What Works

The interviewees are sharp and funny, and the film treats them as people with evidence, not punchlines. Texts pop up on screen as if arriving live, making the pressure feel uncomfortably immediate.

What May Divide Viewers

The polish is both the hook and the problem. Travel-ad visuals and staged recreations make it addictive, but risk turning the debt and heartbreak the women describe into a breezy caper. The man at the center never sits for an interview, yet his phone videos and social posts still give his image plenty of screen time.

Who Should Watch

Made for anyone on dating apps, anyone who has felt pressured to lend money, and friend groups ready for an honest talk about trust and embarrassment.

Your take

Does the dreamy, rom-com-style opening help you see why the women say they trusted him, or does it make the losses they describe too fun to watch?
