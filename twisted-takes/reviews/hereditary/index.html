Hereditary Treats Family Grief as the Scariest Thing in the House

Hereditary (2018)

By Twisted Takes · October 18, 2026 · Spoiler-free

Content advisory: Graphic, disturbing horror imagery, raw grief and family conflict, strong language, drug use and brief graphic nudity.

Premise

After her guarded, difficult mother dies, miniature artist Annie Graham (Toni Collette) tries to hold her household together while untangling her mixed feelings about her. Grief makes everyone stranger, and small, unexplained things start piling up.

The Take

Think of this as a family tragedy that happens to be terrifying. Ari Aster lets old resentments and everything this family won’t say out loud do most of the haunting, so the uncanny seems to grow out of damage already there. It’s the bleakest film in our horror lineup, on purpose.

What Works

Collette is ferocious, swinging from brittle politeness to anguish that’s hard to watch. The opening shot pushes into one of Annie’s model rooms until it becomes real, and the camera keeps framing the family like diorama figures. Colin Stetson’s low, wheezing score simmers underneath like a fever.

What May Divide Viewers

The first hour moves at the speed of mourning, with few conventional jolts. The last stretch shifts gears hard and goes much bigger; some find that thrilling, others miss the grounded family drama.

Who Should Watch

Seasoned horror fans who want to be rattled, and drama lovers who value acting over shocks. Skip it if you’re freshly grieving or want a breezy group watch.

Your take

Is Hereditary scarier as a supernatural story or as a portrait of a family that cannot talk about its pain?
