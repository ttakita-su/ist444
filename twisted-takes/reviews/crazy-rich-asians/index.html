Crazy Rich Asians Is a Glossy Fairy Tale With a Sharp Family Standoff Inside

Crazy Rich Asians (2018)

Content advisory: Some suggestive scenes and jokes, plus scattered swearing.

Premise

Rachel Chu, a New York economics professor, flies to Singapore with boyfriend Nick for his best friend’s wedding. Nick never mentioned that his family is staggeringly rich, or that not everyone will welcome her.

The Take

Nick is charming, yet he’s the least interesting person here. The real story is Rachel decoding a world that has already judged her, led by Nick’s icy mother, Eleanor. Jon M. Chu stages a fairy tale, then lets class tension slice through the sparkle.

What Works

Every surface signals rank. Costumes mark who belongs, a feast at a hawker center (an open-air food court) sells Singapore better than any skyline, and the soundtrack swaps in Chinese-language covers of English pop hits. Amid the gloss, Michelle Yeoh barely raises her voice, and Eleanor is scarier for it; the script gives her reasons, not villainy. Constance Wu keeps Rachel strategic rather than starstruck, and Awkwafina handles the loudest laughs.

What May Divide Viewers

The film wants you to gasp at the mansions and judge them at once. Some will enjoy that; others will feel the camera adores the excess more than it questions it.

Who Should Watch

If you’ve ever felt quietly evaluated at a partner’s family dinner, this one gets it.

Your take

Who is more compelling: Rachel, the outsider fighting for a seat at the table, or Eleanor, the matriarch guarding it?
