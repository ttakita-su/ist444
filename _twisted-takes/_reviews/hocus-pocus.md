## Premise

In 1693 Salem, the three Sanderson sisters are hanged for witchcraft, and a boy who tried to stop them is cursed to live forever as a black cat. Three hundred years later, new kid Max (Omri Katz) takes his little sister Dani (Thora Birch) trick-or-treating, scoffs at the local legend and accidentally brings the witches back for one very long Halloween night.

## The Take

Hocus Pocus is two movies stitched together, and only one of them is good. The kids-on-an-adventure half is thin. The witches half is a gleeful, scenery-chewing comedy, and it's the reason a 1993 box-office disappointment turned into an October ritual. Every time Bette Midler, Sarah Jessica Parker and Kathy Najimy share the frame, the movie comes alive.

## What Works

Midler plays head witch Winifred like a diva who's been offstage for three centuries and means to make up for it: buck teeth, hissing vowels and perfect timing. Parker's sing-song Sarah and Najimy's sniffing, eager-to-please Mary round out a trio that bickers like real sisters. Director Kenny Ortega, a choreographer by trade, turns Midler's "I Put a Spell on You" at the town Halloween party into a full showstopper. The production design does the rest: crooked cottages, candlelit spell books and a Salem that looks like autumn in a snow globe.

## What May Divide Viewers

Max is a bland hero, and whenever the film cuts away from the sisters, momentum sags. The talking cat was early digital effects work and looks it now. Some jokes, especially a running gag about Max's love life, land oddly in a family film. Watched cold, without the nostalgia, it can feel more like a sugar rush than a movie.

## Who Should Watch

Families with kids around eight and up, Halloween-party crowds who want something on in the background that everyone can quote, and anyone who grew up on its October TV reruns. Horror fans looking for actual scares should keep walking.
